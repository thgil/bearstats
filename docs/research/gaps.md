## 1. Claims still resting on secondary sources or on no source

Ordered by how much of the page depends on them.

1. **"Licensed bear hunters number fewer than 20,000, down from more than 70,000 in the 1970s"** (`webapp/index.html` line 195, footer line 208 attributes it to "Japan Times, Britannica"). No source anywhere. Not in `baseline/britannica-wb.html` (grep for 70,000 and 20,000 returns nothing), not in either Japan Times snapshot, not in any MoE file. Contradicted by MoE `baseline/syubetu.pdf` (517,800 in 1975, 213,400 in 2021; gun licences 493,700 to 84,400). Every research doc already says remove it. It is still on the page.

2. **"Bear population ... about 54,000, up from about 15,000 in 2012"** (`index.html` line 195). Both numbers are Britannica's. Britannica's own sentence (read from `baseline/britannica-wb.html`) is "combined bear numbers from 15,000 individuals in 2012 to an estimated 54,000 in 2025", with no source given. The research docs' explanation that the 15,000 is the lower bound of the 2012 manual's black-bear range is an inference; Britannica does not say so and calls it a combined figure. The 2012 manual (`baseline/tebiki-tougou-2012.pdf`) gives two different black-bear ranges, 15,000 to 20,000 in the body text (page text line "ツキノワグマは 1 万 5 千頭から 2 万頭程度") and 13,000 to 21,000 in table 3-1, plus 16,000 as a 2006 internal MoE figure; the research docs use both ranges interchangeably without saying which is which.

3. **The replacement population figure, 57,308, is not an MoE number either.** It is the research's own sum of all 25 rows of table Ⅱ-２ in the draft guideline (`moe/extracted/population-by-prefecture.csv`; sum re-checked today). The table mixes prefecture rows with regional-population rows that cover the same ground: 北近畿（東部）(福井県・滋賀県・京都府) 1,166 sits alongside separate rows for 福井県 1,217, 滋賀県 316 and 京都府 1,639; 北近畿（西部）(京都府・兵庫県) 762 overlaps 京都府 again; 東中国 (兵庫県・鳥取県・岡山県) 763 is the only Hyogo figure. Whether these overlap or are disjoint management units is not stated in the table, so the sum may double count. Eight prefectures are marked ※3 (unpublished), so it also undercounts. Footnote ※2 of the table says outright that estimates cannot be simply compared across years because methods differ. The same applies to "45,708 black bears" and "all 16 prefectures rose". These should be presented as "sum of published prefectural estimates, surveys 2020 to 2024, overlapping units not resolved", never as "MoE estimates 57,000".

4. **"The cause was a failed acorn and beechnut crop"** (`index.html` lines 82, 197) is attributed on the page to Japan Times and Britannica (line 208). The primary source is on disk (`mast/buna-r7-fruiting.pdf`, Tohoku office, 2025-11-06) and is not cited. The acorn half rested on Fukushima's categorical table only; as of today it also has Toyama's 2025 primary (`gaps/toyama-r7houkyou.pdf`: ブナ 凶作, ミズナラ 不作, コナラ 不作). Still no oak series for Akita or Iwate.

5. **The "failure year" classification for FY2019 to FY2022 in `cross-mast-vs-outbreak.md`** rests on the Miyagi chart's six-point proxy, and the primary contradicts it. The Tohoku office's own autumn results, fetched today:
   - R2 (2020, `gaps/tohoku-buna-27.pdf`): 青森 2.3 並作, 岩手 1.3 凶作, 宮城 0.7 大凶作, 秋田 2.0 並作, 山形 0.3 大凶作 (142 points).
   - R3 (2021, `gaps/tohoku-buna-29.pdf`): 青森 1.0 凶作, 岩手 0.7 大凶作, 宮城 1.7 凶作, 秋田 0.2 大凶作, 山形 1.5 凶作 (142 points, 3 not surveyed).
   So 2020, classed as a failure year, was 並作 in Akita and Aomori; 2021, classed as a non-failure year, was 大凶作 in Akita (0.2, the same grade as 2023's 0.1 and 2025's 0.0) and Iwate. Akita's October sightings were 116 in 2020 and 100 in 2021 (`docs/research/cross-weather-vs-mast.csv`) against 1,472 in 2023 and 5,810 in 2025. The "failure vs other" means (11,788 vs 2,520 Oct+Nov sightings; 193 vs 83 injured) and the sentence in `stats-and-claims.md` claim 5 that uses them must be recomputed on the primary index. The draft guideline's own text (`gaps/guideline-r08.txt`, section (2) 出没状況) says the 2019 and 2020 outbreaks were centred on Hokuriku and the 2023 and 2025 ones on Tohoku, which is a different story from "Tohoku mast failure in 2019 and 2020".

6. **GOAL.md claim 6 (weather drives mast)** has no supporting source. `cross-weather-vs-mast.md` tests it and finds the sign reversed (prior-summer temperature rho +0.45, n = 16). The mechanism paper cited (Kon 2010, `weather/kon_2010_masting_review.pdf`) points to previous-spring minimum temperature, which was not tested (only a monthly-mean proxy). "Masaka and Sato 2002" is a citation inside Kon, never located. The page should not say anything causal about weather.

7. **"Most on record"** for sightings (`index.html` line 28) rests on FY2013 onward. The Wayback CDX (`gaps/wayback-cdx-syutubotu.txt`) shows the earliest archived `syutubotu.pdf` is 2016-12-23 (already held), so no earlier edition can be recovered from that URL. The draft guideline (`gaps/guideline-r08.txt`) does describe FY2023 as 過去最高 before FY2025, which supports "record" from MoE's own mouth, but only as text.

8. **Secondary figures quoted in the research docs that were never read from a fetched page**: Japan Times 2025-12-26 "around 220,000 licensed hunters ... 532,265 registered in 1970" (search snippet; grep of `baseline/jt-20251226-wb.html` finds neither number, the article is paywalled); Britannica "58,000 by early 2026" (search snippet); Fujiki 2018 "R squared 0.96" (Semantic Scholar abstract); Oka 2004 "5 of 7 Tohoku zones" (search snippet); the Hokkoku Shimbun Toyama story (now superseded by the primary, see section 2).

9. `design-directions.md` marks Reuters, Bloomberg and SCMP as blocked and its statements about them as guesses. They are not data claims, but they should not be quoted as observations.
