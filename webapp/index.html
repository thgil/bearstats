“Last year” is fiscal 2025, April 2025 to March 2026. “This year” is fiscal 2026, which began in April: its sightings run to 30 June 2026, injuries and deaths to 31 July. All counts are reports made to the Ministry of the Environment — encounters, not bears.

01

What happened last year?

Twice the sightings of any year before and thirteen people killed — most of it in one autumn, in one region.

Four years on one map.

Bear sightings reported in each prefecture, fiscal 2022 to fiscal 2025. Each year runs April to the following March. Watch the north.

It had a centre.

In fiscal 2025, Akita and Iwate alone reported 23,331 of the country’s 50,801 sightings — nearly half. Raw counts flatter big rural prefectures, but the gap here is not size.

It happened in autumn 2025.

Most years sightings peak in June and fall away. In October 2025 alone there were 15,998 — more than the whole of fiscal 2022. The beech and oak crop had failed across Tohoku; bears that would have fed in the hills came down for apples, persimmons and rubbish, and found towns. In November the government sent soldiers to Akita.

The same year, report by report.

Akita and Iwate publish totals only. Four prefectures further south — Toyama, Niigata, Gunma and Saitama — publish every report with a location. This is their fiscal 2025, one month at a time: 64 reports in April 2025, 1,795 in October. Yellow marks are people hurt.

Thirteen dead.

Thirteen people were killed by bears between April 2025 and March 2026. The previous worst since the ministry’s count began in 2008 was six.

02

Is it happening again this year?

So far, yes. Every month of 2026 has run well above the same month of the record year.

April to June, this year against last.

April 2026: 1,787 sightings, against 800 in April 2025. May: 4,581 against 2,528. June: 6,260 against 4,227.

67% ahead at the end of June.

By 30 June 2026 this year had logged 12,628 sightings. At the same point of the record year: 7,555.

More bears seen. Not, yet, more people hurt.

April to July 2026: 53 people injured, against 55 in the same months of 2025. Six killed, against four — a gap too small to read anything into.

03

Will this autumn be worse?

Nobody knows yet. It depends on the acorn crop, and that only shows from September.

A fast spring does not mean a bad autumn. Fiscal 2024 was running ahead of fiscal 2025 at the end of June — 7,601 sightings against 7,555 — and finished at 20,513, 40% of the record year’s total. Those two years are the chart below.

What has changed is the baseline underneath both years. Japan's bear population roughly tripled between 2012 and 2025, from an estimated 15,000 to around 54,000. Over the same years the people licensed to hunt them aged out of the job: more than 70,000 active bear hunters in the 1970s, fewer than 20,000 by 2025. More bears, fewer hunters — that does not reset every April.

What decides whether autumn 2026 repeats autumn 2025 is the mast crop — the beech and oak nuts bears fatten on before winter. A second failed crop would send hungry bears back into towns; a good one would ease the pressure. The trees fruit from September, so the first read on the crop comes now, and the reports through October will settle it.

Check back in November.

Methods

A “sighting” here is any report made to a prefectural government of a bear seen, tracked, or otherwise encountered — not a confirmed animal. The same bear can generate several reports, and reporting rates vary with how alarmed people are, so year-on-year swings partly reflect that as well as bear activity.

Only four prefectures — Toyama, Niigata, Gunma and Saitama — publish individual reports as open data; the other 42, including Akita and Iwate, publish totals only. Hokkaido's point-data feed begins in May 2026 and is excluded from the month-by-month replay above.
