01

What happened in fiscal 2025?

50,801 sightings, 2.1 times the previous high. 238 people injured and 13 killed. Half of it in October and November, and three quarters of that in Tohoku.

Thirteen years of sightings.

The Ministry's series begins in fiscal 2013 with 9,133 sightings. Fiscal 2023 set a record of 24,348. Fiscal 2025 more than doubled it: 50,801.

238 injured, 13 killed.

Both are the highest since the injury count began in 2008. The previous highs were 219 injured and 6 killed, both in fiscal 2023.

Two prefectures, one autumn.

Akita reported 13,592 sightings in fiscal 2025 and Iwate 9,739, 46% of the national total. In October alone the two reported 8,898 of 15,998.

The same year, month by month.

Each cell is one prefecture in one month. October and November 2025 were 26,336 sightings, 52% of the year. The same two months of fiscal 2024 were 3,518.

Report by report.

Toyama, Niigata, Gunma and Saitama publish each report with a location as open data. Akita does too, through its クマダス system, and those reports will join this map in a later update. The map plays their fiscal 2025 month by month: 64 reports in April, 1,795 in October. Yellow marks are people injured.

02

Why do some autumns explode?

When the beech crop fails. The six largest Octobers since 2013 all followed a poor crop, and a good crop is almost always followed by a poor one.

Beech crop and October sightings, 2012 to 2025.

Top row: the Tohoku Regional Forest Office beech index, 0 to 5, from up to 145 survey points in five prefectures. Bottom row: national October sightings. The six largest Octobers all came in years with an index below 2. No year graded 並作 or better produced an October above 2,235. The index says which years spike, not how big: 2014, 2016 and 2019 had indices as low as 2023 and Octobers near 2,600. In 2023 the index was 0.06 and October had 5,983 sightings. In 2025, 0.10 and 15,998.

A good crop is followed by a bad one.

Across the five prefectures, an autumn graded 並作 or 豊作 was followed by a poor one in 22 of 23 cases since 2012. All seven 豊作 years were followed by 大凶作. A poor year says nothing about the next: 21 of 42 were followed by another poor year. 2024 was good across Tohoku. 2025 failed.

Weather does not explain it.

Summer temperature and rainfall at Akita in the year before a crop show no relationship with the crop over 16 years. The 2023 and 2025 failures coincided with the two hottest summers, but the July survey had already graded both years 大凶作 before the heat.

03

Will autumn 2026 repeat autumn 2025?

Probably not. Spring was far ahead, but spring does not set the autumn, the beech crop is forecast good, and in every prefecture with comparable data July and August were below 2025.

The busiest spring in 14 years.

April to June 2026: 12,628 sightings. The previous high was 7,601 in fiscal 2024. Fiscal 2025 had 7,555 at the same point.

The surge did not last the summer.

Three prefectures and one group publish their counts ahead of the ministry. Akita, July 2026: 904 sightings, against 1,055 in July 2025. August: 251 against 766. Miyagi: 222 against 250, then 132 against 225. Toyama, Niigata, Gunma and Saitama together: 363 against 420, then 134 against 320. Iwate changed how it counts in April 2026 and cannot be compared. The ministry’s own July figures are due in September.

Spring does not set the autumn.

Across 13 years, October plus November ranged from a quarter of the spring count to three and a half times it. A high spring sets a floor for the year, not the autumn peak. Applying each past year's shape to 12,628 gives a full year of 28,293 to 84,913. That is arithmetic, not a forecast.

The 2026 crop is forecast good.

Tohoku Regional Forest Office, 7 July 2026: 豊作 in Iwate, Miyagi, Akita and Yamagata, 並作 in Aomori. Mean index 3.9, the highest July figure in 15 years, against 0.44 on the same date in 2025 and 0.54 in 2023. Niigata, 189 points: 豊作 in all four regions. Toyama: 豊作, the first in its 12-year series. Akita's five sites: full crop at four. Since 2012 the July forecast matched the autumn grade in 48 of 70 prefecture-years and was within one grade in 68. Where it missed, the autumn came in worse. A July forecast of 並作 or better has never ended in 大凶作 in 31 cases. It slipped to 凶作 eight times.

Injuries are ordinary. Deaths are not.

April to July 2026: 53 people injured, within the range of the last ten years (31 to 56). Six killed, the most for that window since the monthly count began in 2016.

04

Why are there more bears than there were?

Estimates rose in every prefecture with two surveys, and gun licences fell 83% since 1975.

Methods

What a sighting is

A sighting is a report made to a prefectural government of a bear seen, tracked or otherwise encountered. It is not a count of bears: one bear can produce several reports. The Ministry of the Environment compiles the prefectures' counts into one provisional table each month and revises earlier months in later editions. Prefectures compile reports by different methods (police, municipalities, or both), so counts are not comparable between prefectures.

Reporting effects

Reporting rises when people are alarmed. After a year with 13 deaths, more people report bears and more prefectures log traces. The data cannot separate that from bears, so part of any year-on-year change is reporting.

The beech index

The Tohoku Regional Forest Office surveys beech only, at fixed points in Aomori, Iwate, Miyagi, Akita and Yamagata (up to 145 points). Its own definition of the index, quoted: “区分 / 開花（結実）状況 / 豊凶指数: 全体 樹冠全体にたくさんの花（実）がついている ５; 部分 樹冠上部に多くの花（実）がついている ３; 一部 ごくわずかに花（実）がついている １; 非開花（非結実） まったく花（実）がついていない ０. 結実予測は、各調査箇所の調査結果を数値化、集計し豊凶指数を算出して、下表のとおり結実の豊凶を推測します。 豊凶指数 3.5以上 豊作 / 2.0以上3.5未満 並作 / 1.0以上2.0未満 凶作 / 1.0未満 大凶作. 注：豊凶指数1.0未満でも一部に開花・結実が見られる場合もあり、誤解を避けるため、平成29年度から豊凶区分の「皆無」を「大凶作」に変更しました。 (東北森林管理局. The prefecture index is the mean of the per-point scores over the points surveyed that year.)” The July flowering survey gives the forecast; the autumn fruiting survey gives the result. Both use the same points and the same index.

Point data

Five prefectures publish individual reports as open data: Toyama, Niigata, Gunma, Saitama and, through its クマダス system, Akita (CC BY 4.0, records with coordinates from October 2023). Akita’s reports are not yet on the map. Iwate, Miyagi and most other prefectures publish totals; the ministry compiles them about six weeks after month end. The summer panel uses Akita, Miyagi and Iwate’s own publications for 2026 and the ministry’s table for 2025. Hokkaido’s feed began in May 2026 and is not in the month-by-month replay.

What this page does not claim

It does not say that weather causes the beech crop to fail: at Akita, the previous summer's temperature and rainfall show no relationship with the crop over 16 years. It does not say anything about acorns outside Fukushima and Toyama, because no other prefecture on file publishes an oak series. It does not draw a national bear population line, because there is no national series: the Ministry tabulates prefectural surveys taken in different years by different methods.
